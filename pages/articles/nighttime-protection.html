Hair Care

Nighttime Protection: Silk, Satin, and Hair Wrapping Traditions

Night is when unprotected textured hair loses the most — to friction, to dryness, to styles that unravel on cotton pillowcases.

In many African households, covering the hair before sleep was ordinary wisdom: scarves, wraps, and careful tying of styles so the morning crown still looked intentional. Today we name the science — friction and moisture loss — but the practice is ancestral.

Why cotton works against coils

Cotton absorbs moisture and creates rough friction. Coils already struggle to stay hydrated; a night on cotton can mean frizz, tangles and breakage at the edges and ends.

Silk and satin as modern allies

Silk and satin pillowcases or bonnets reduce friction and help seal in the products you applied by day. They are not vanity. They are retention tools — especially for braids, twists, locs and wash-and-go styles.

Wrapping as tradition and technique

Wrapping can mean a bonnet, a scarf tied with care, or pineapple-ing an afro so the roots are not crushed. The method matters less than the habit: protect the style, protect the ends, wake up with less repair work.

When you wrap at night, you join a long line of people who treated rest as part of the hair ritual — not an afterthought.
