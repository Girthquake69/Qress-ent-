Decolonizing Beauty: Redefining Professionalism in Corporate Spaces

“Professional” was never a neutral word. For African hair, it often meant closer to European texture, until people pushed back.

African hair is not a problem to solve. Yet workplaces and schools have long treated locs, afros, braids and twists as exceptions that need permission.

The costume of professionalism

Dress codes that ban “unusual hairstyles” while allowing straight, loose hair encode bias. They ask African employees to spend money, time and health approximating another standard.

Law and culture shifting

Crown-act-style legislation in various countries and states names hair discrimination explicitly. Culture moves too: leaders who wear natural styles in public make room for everyone else.

What decolonizing looks like day to day

It looks like hiring without texture prejudice, training managers, and refusing shame in the mirror before a meeting. Professionalism is competence and care, not chemical straightness.
