Hair Care

The Science of Porosity and Coily Hair Retention

Porosity is not a trend label. It is how your hair’s cuticle behaves — and it explains why the same product can leave one crown soft and another still thirsty.

Coily and kinky textures already have a unique architecture: tight bends that make it harder for natural scalp oils to travel down the shaft. Porosity layers a second truth on top of that structure.

What porosity actually means

Low-porosity hair has tightly bound cuticles. Water and product sit on the surface longer; steam, warmth and lighter layers help. High-porosity hair has more open or lifted cuticles — often from heat, colour, or genetics — so it drinks quickly and loses moisture just as fast.

High porosity: fill and seal

If your hair feels dry within hours of washing, prioritise protein–moisture balance and sealing. Creams and butters can help fill gaps; oils lock the hydration in. Protective styles reduce daily handling that snaps weakened strands.

Low porosity: patience and heat

If product seems to “sit” and hair stays stiff, simplify. Use lighter leave-ins, apply on damp hair, and allow gentle warmth (a warm towel or steamer) so cuticles open enough to accept moisture. Heavy butters alone may only coat the outside.

Retention is the real goal

Length is mostly what you keep, not only what you grow. A porosity-aware routine — correct water, correct seal, fewer harsh practices — is how African hair retains its crown across seasons and years.
