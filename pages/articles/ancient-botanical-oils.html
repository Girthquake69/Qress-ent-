Hair Care

Ancient Botanical Oils: Chebe, Marula, and Baobab

Long before laboratory serums, African communities refined oils from seed, fruit and earth — formulas that still speak to coily hair better than most modern shortcuts.

Across the Sahel, the savanna and the rainforest belt, hair care was never separate from land. Families pressed, infused and shared oils as part of daily life — not as luxury accessories, but as knowledge passed through hands.

Chebe: the Chadian secret of length

Chebe is a traditional mixture associated with the women of Chad, often linked with the ability to grow and retain remarkable length. Recipes vary, but many include local plant powders, oils and a careful sealing method applied after washing. What matters culturally is the discipline: regular application, protective styling, and treating hair as something to be preserved, not constantly stripped.

For modern textured hair, the lesson is less about chasing one “miracle jar” and more about consistent sealing — locking moisture in after water-based hydration so coils do not dry out between wash days.

Marula: soft strength

Marula oil, pressed from the fruit of the marula tree native to southern Africa, is prized for its lightness and high fatty-acid profile. It absorbs without the heavy film some oils leave, making it useful for fine-to-medium coils that need slip without collapse.

Used sparingly on damp hair or as a scalp massage oil, marula supports softness and shine while remaining true to a regional botanical story — one rooted in African ecology rather than imported trends.

Baobab: the tree of life on the strand

Baobab oil carries the symbolism of the baobab itself: endurance, storage, generosity. Rich in vitamins and emollients, it helps reduce the rough feel of high-porosity hair and supports elasticity — the quiet defence against breakage.

When you choose baobab, marula or chebe-inspired routines, you are not only moisturising. You are aligning care with ingredients that grew beside the communities who first understood African hair as something to honour, not “fix.”

A simple ancestral-inspired routine

Cleanse gently. Hydrate with water or a light leave-in. Seal with a few drops of oil, focusing on ends. Protect at night. Repeat with patience. The botanicals matter — the consistency matters more.
